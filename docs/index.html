Your PO talks. ScrumScribe writes the backlog.

From a Product Owner's words to scoped, testable Jira tickets. ScrumScribe reads what already exists in Jira, Confluence and the repo, asks only for what is missing, and splits the work into lean, testable stories. It posts only after the PO and Scrum Master approve, and a hook asks a human before any change to Jira or Confluence.

Built for the gap between product and engineering

Most ticket problems start before the sprint: vague criteria, scope that keeps growing, and tests added at the end. These are the four things the harness does about it.

Reads before it asks

Pulls context from Jira, Confluence and the repo first. The PO only answers what the sources don't, five questions at a time.

Keeps scope lean

Sets a time budget first, splits by user value with SPIDR, and lists every cut so nothing disappears quietly.

Designs tests up front

Acceptance criteria come as concrete examples. Integration, eval and UAT tickets appear only when a trigger applies.

Humans stay in charge

Helpers are read-only. The PO approves scope, the Scrum Master approves owners and posting, and a hook asks before any Atlassian change.

How the pieces connect

One orchestrator talks to people. Six helper agents read and draft. Only the orchestrator can write to Jira or Confluence, and only after a yes.

Five stages, two human gates

The harness loops on its own until the gaps are closed and the scope fits the appetite. Then two people must approve before anything is posted.

1

Discover

Reads Jira, Confluence and the repo. Every fact gets a source link.

2

Gap check

Marks each area of the question bank Known, Inferred or Missing.

3

Ask

At most five questions per round, multiple choice where possible, each saying why it matters.

4

Draft

Requirements, lean stories, test and eval tickets, a red-team review and a house-style rewrite.

5

Hand off

PO approval, owner suggestions from the team file, second approval (Scrum Master by default), then posting with links back.

Install and run

You need Claude Code, opened in your project folder. Reading and posting need an Atlassian MCP connection for Jira Cloud and Confluence Cloud. Without it, everything is drafted locally for you to paste.

# 1. Add the marketplace and install the plugin (inside Claude Code)
/plugin marketplace add shmouses/scrumscribe
/plugin install scrumscribe@scrumscribe

# 2. Connect Jira and Confluence, then sign in when prompted (or via /mcp)
/plugin install atlassian@claude-plugins-official
/reload-plugins

# 3. Tailor it to your team
/scrumscribe:setup we run two-week Scrum, epics and stories, we want a PRD and ADRs

# 4. Start a session
/scrumscribe:po Finance wants to export invoices without asking engineering

See the worked example for what each stage produces.

Safety you can check

Two guarantees are enforced by how the parts are wired. The rest are rules the orchestrator follows, and every step leaves a record you can read.

  • Enforced: helpers cannot write. Their tools are limited to reading files and loading skills. No MCP tools, no shell. CI checks this.
  • Enforced: a hook asks first. Any Atlassian tool that is not a known read asks a human before it runs. Unknown new tools ask too.
  • Followed: two recorded approvals. The PO and the Scrum Master (or the role your team names) each sign off, in their own words, in approvals.md.
  • Followed: suggest, never assign. Owner suggestions come from the team file. The Scrum Master decides.
  • Minimal data. Credentials stay in Claude Code. The team file holds a name or handle, skills, ownership and capacity.
  • A full audit trail. Each session leaves plain-text records you can read, diff and keep.

Tailor it to any organization

Two small files, owned by the team, decide the method, ticket levels, documents, writing style and limits. Or describe how you work in plain English and approve the result.

FileHolds
charter.yamlMethod, sprint length, ticket levels, Definition of Ready and Done, documents wanted, limits, who gives the second approval
team.yamlPods, what each owns, people's work skills (1 to 3), accountability, capacity
# .scrumscribe/charter.yaml (excerpt)
method: scrum
sprint_length_days: 10
hierarchy: [epic, story, subtask]
docs_wanted: [prd, fr-nfr-spec, adr, test-plan]
limits:
  stories_per_epic: 8
  acceptance_criteria_per_story: 5
approvals:
  second_approver_role: scrum-master
